Asset Classes

Investments

Educational coverage of major asset classes — what they are, how they work, principal risks, and portfolio context.

Understanding asset classes is foundational to portfolio construction. The descriptions below are educational.

Equities

Ownership shares in companies. Prices reflect supply, demand, earnings expectations, and market conditions. Principal risks: company-specific, market, liquidity, and permanent capital loss.

Fixed Income & Bonds

Debt instruments that typically pay interest and return principal at maturity. Bond prices move inversely with interest rates. Risks: interest-rate, credit, inflation, and liquidity.

Foreign Exchange

The market for exchanging currencies. Rates driven by economic conditions, interest rates, and capital flows. High leverage and geopolitical risk for speculative positions.

Commodities (including Gold)

Physical goods traded as futures, spot, or related instruments. Gold is often used as a store of value and diversifier. Risks: volatility, storage/roll costs, geopolitical shocks.

Market Indices

Statistical measures of security baskets (e.g. S&P 500, NASDAQ-100). Enable broad market exposure via funds and derivatives.

Derivatives

Contracts whose value derives from an underlying — options, futures, forwards, swaps. Used for hedging or speculation. Risks: complexity, leverage, counterparty risk.

Real Estate

Physical property or claims on property income, including REITs. Risks: illiquidity, concentration, interest-rate sensitivity.

Private Markets & Alternatives

Private equity, private credit, infrastructure, hedge funds. Capital typically locked multi-year. Risks: illiquidity, valuation uncertainty, manager risk.

Digital Assets

Cryptocurrencies including Bitcoin. Extreme volatility, regulatory uncertainty, and custody risk. If included, typically a small risk-budgeted allocation.
