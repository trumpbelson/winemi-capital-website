Zone quality checklist

A zone never revisited since formation often carries more informational weight than one chopped through many times. Each revisit can fill residual orders. Freshness is a quality score — not a promise of reversal.
Impulsive, wide-range candles leaving a tight base suggest imbalance. Slow drifts away from a messy base are weaker evidence. Prefer departure that also breaks nearby structure where possible.
A lower-timeframe demand zone inside a higher-timeframe discount area is a different hypothesis than the same zone against a higher-timeframe supply wall. Context filters noise.
  • Origin: zones form at the base of an impulsive move; S/R often forms from repeated turns.
  • Retests: untested zones can be preferred; repeated S/R tests sometimes strengthen a level in classic TA.
  • Order-flow idea: zones aim to mark imbalance left by size; S/R is primarily price history.

Order flow — practical observables

Order flow is the sequence of aggressive and passive orders. Retail charts rarely show a bank’s ticket; they show effects.

  • Time & sales: sequence of prints — useful for spotting bursts of size.
  • Depth / book: resting liquidity — thin vs thick areas.
  • Footprint / volume profile: where volume clustered inside a bar or session.
  • Delta / absorption ideas: whether aggressive flow was accepted or absorbed (hypothesis only).
Information asymmetry is real: large blocks, hedges, and off-screen activity may not be visible. Treat order-flow language as a way to describe observables, not as proof of intent on every candle.

Risk framing

A complete setup is still a probability statement. Define invalidation beyond the zone, size risk before entry ideas, and accept that many zones fail. Education improves recognition; it does not remove uncertainty.
