Macro & data desk

Policy paths, growth dispersion, and the data calendar — read next to structure, not instead of it. This desk is educational context only; it is not a forecast or a solicitation.

How to use this desk

Scan the calendar for high-impact releases. Note whether quiet bases sit in front of those events. Cross-check equities against yields, the dollar, and gold on the overview and mini charts. Invalidation still belongs on the price structure — macro only sets the backdrop.

Desk workflow

  1. Calendar first. Identify high-impact releases in the next 1–5 sessions (CPI, labour, PMIs, central banks).
  2. Map the backdrop. Check US 10Y, DXY, and major equity indices for regime — risk-on, risk-off, or mixed.
  3. Structure second. Only then open HTF zones and candles. Event risk is part of zone quality.
  4. Write invalidation. If a base sits into a major print, define what would break the structure before the print, not after.

Study themes

Policy reaction function

How markets price the next moves in rates often matters as much as the level of rates itself. Watch front-end yields and terminal-rate narratives around major prints.

Growth dispersion

Regions diverge. Relative growth shows up in FX, equity leadership, and commodity demand. Compare US, Europe, and Asia rather than treating “risk” as one switch.

Event risk

Treat major data and central-bank days as part of zone quality — not an afterthought. Quiet bases into those events can fail when liquidity reprices.

Dollar & yields

A rising DXY with rising real yields often pressures gold and emerging FX; the reverse can support them. Always confirm with structure on the chart you trade.

Liquidity lens

Balance-sheet paths and funding stress change the amplitude of moves. Macro does not replace candle invalidation; it changes how far a failed level may run.

Cross-asset check

Equities, yields, the dollar, and gold should be read together. Isolated charts invite narrative; the desk prefers convergent context.
