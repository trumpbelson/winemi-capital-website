Research

Insights

Substantive explanations of markets, investment, macroeconomics, portfolio construction, risk, and technology — written to teach, not to sell.

Insights is a research and financial knowledge publication. Each category explains what the subject means, why it matters, and how it relates to markets and capital.

  • Market Insights — Price movements, liquidity, volatility, cycles, and the forces that move financial assets. Understanding market mechanics helps interpret price action without confusing noise for signal.
  • Investment Research — Fundamental analysis, valuation, financial statements, industry structure, and investment theses. Research reduces the role of opinion and increases the role of evidence.
  • Macroeconomics — Inflation, interest rates, GDP, employment, monetary and fiscal policy, and currencies. Asset prices respond to the economic environment.
  • Wealth Creation — Accumulation, preservation, compounding, diversification, and financial discipline. Without a framework, short-term decisions can undermine long-term security.
  • Portfolio Management — Construction, allocation, correlation, rebalancing, liquidity, and risk-adjusted performance. Most investors experience markets through portfolios, not single positions.
  • Trading & Market Structure — Price action, technical analysis, support and resistance, liquidity, and trading psychology. These are analytical concepts, not guaranteed methods of predicting prices.
  • Technology & Artificial Intelligence — Machine learning, quantitative systems, data, automation, and model limitations in finance. Understanding capabilities and limitations prevents over-reliance on systems that can fail in new regimes.
  • Economics & Business — Business models, corporate performance, accounting, and capital allocation inside firms. Equity returns ultimately depend on business performance.
  • Accounting & Financial Analysis — Financial statements, ratios, cash flow, liquidity, solvency, and profitability metrics. Without the ability to read statements, investment analysis rests on incomplete information.
  • Risk Management — Identification, position sizing, drawdowns, liquidity risk, and the gap between return and acceptable risk. Returns without risk control are incomplete.
