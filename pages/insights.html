Structure · Liquidity

When bases matter more than headlines

Impulsive departures from compact bases often carry more information than the narrative of the day. Quality rises with prior impulse strength, tight bases, HTF acceptance, and clear invalidation.

Structure

The four patterns — a working vocabulary

DBR and RBR describe demand contexts; RBD and DBD describe supply. Labels are shared language for imbalance — not trade signals. Filter with higher-timeframe structure.

Macro · Liquidity

Policy paths and the price of liquidity

Rate expectations, the dollar, and balance-sheet signals set the backdrop for equity and commodity structure. Quiet bases into policy events can fail violently.

Risk

Invalidation is a design choice

Where the idea is wrong should be written before where it might work. Size follows from that distance and how many failed ideas the book can absorb.

Liquidity · Structure

Absorption versus exhaustion

Not every pause is accumulation. Departure quality and follow-through help distinguish absorption from a move that is simply out of fuel.

Macro

Growth dispersion across regions

Economies sit in different cycle phases. Relative growth shows up in FX, regional equity leadership, and commodity demand.

Risk · Structure

Crowding and narrow leadership

An index that holds up while breadth deteriorates is different from a broad advance. Concentration changes how shocks transmit.

Macro · Risk

Event risk is part of zone quality

A perfect base into CPI, NFP, or a central-bank decision is not the same setup as identical geometry on a quiet data day.

Structure

Higher-timeframe context first

Map weekly and daily structure before refining intraday patterns. Sequence: regime → bases → location → confirmation → invalidation.

Liquidity

Session liquidity and narrative leadership

Asia, Europe, and North America rotate depth and narrative. Structure still comes first; session context changes expected noise.
