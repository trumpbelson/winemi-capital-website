Why structure matters more than prediction

Markets are continuous auctions. Price expands when one side overwhelms available liquidity and contracts when two-way interest balances. Supply and demand education focuses on recognising those transitions — rally, base, drop — rather than forecasting news headlines.

Institutional order flow in plain language

Large participants cannot always enter or exit at a single print without moving the market. Their activity often leaves structural clues:

  • Strong departures from a base (imbalance)
  • Failed breaks beyond obvious highs/lows (liquidity runs)
  • Defended levels on a first retest (possible residual interest)

Order flow is not a crystal ball. It is a lens for reading where risk and reward may be asymmetric if the prior imbalance still has force.

Supply & demand: a practical vocabulary

Shared language reduces ambiguity. Common terms include RBR, RBD, DBR, DBD; demand and supply zones; higher-time-frame location; and “complete setup” discipline — only acting when structure, location, and confirmation align.

Educational programmes in this tradition emphasise practice and review of historical price action so pattern recognition becomes literate, not impulsive.

Risk is the product

Return without risk control is incomplete. Position sizing, maximum loss per idea, correlation, and the possibility of regime change belong in every serious process — discretionary or systematic.
