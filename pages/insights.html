Structure · Liquidity

When bases matter more than headlines

Impulsive departures from compact bases often carry more information than the narrative of the day. Quality rises with prior impulse strength, a tight orderly base, higher-timeframe acceptance, and a clear invalidation level written before targets.

Practise sequence: mark HTF regime → locate the base → score departure → define what proves the idea wrong.

Macro · Liquidity

Policy paths and the price of liquidity

Rate expectations, the dollar, and balance-sheet signals set the backdrop against which equity and commodity structure plays out. Quiet bases into major policy events can fail violently when liquidity reprices.

Treat the economic calendar as part of zone quality. Cross-check equities against yields, DXY, and gold.

Risk

Invalidation is a design choice

Where the idea is wrong should be written before where it might work. Position size follows from that distance and from how many consecutive failed ideas the book can absorb.

In elevated-VIX environments, demand stronger departures and clearer HTF alignment.

Structure

The four patterns — working vocabulary

DBR and RBR describe demand; RBD and DBD describe supply. Labels are language — not signals. Filter with HTF structure.

Liquidity · Structure

Absorption versus exhaustion

Not every pause is accumulation. Departure quality and follow-through distinguish absorption from a move out of fuel.

Macro

Growth dispersion across regions

Relative growth shows up in FX, regional equity leadership, and commodity demand. Session leadership rotates with depth.

Risk · Structure

Crowding and narrow leadership

An index that holds while breadth deteriorates is different from a broad advance. Concentration changes shock transmission.

Macro · Risk

Event risk is part of zone quality

A perfect base into CPI, NFP, or a central-bank decision is not the same as identical geometry on a quiet data day.
