Anatomy of a candle

Each candle summarises open, high, low, and close for a session. The body is the distance between open and close. The wicks show how far price traded beyond that range before the period closed.

  • Bullish close — close above open (buyers held the period).
  • Bearish close — close below open (sellers held the period).
  • Long body — strong directional commitment during the session.
  • Long wick — rejection or exploration that failed to hold.
  • Doji-like — open and close nearly equal; indecision relative to the range.

Context before labels

Higher-timeframe first

A hammer at HTF support after a clear discount differs from the same shape in a choppy mid-range with no structure.

Location matters

Rejection wicks into premium or discount relative to the HTF swing change the quality of the read. Geometry alone is not enough.

Invalidation

Define what proves the idea wrong before treating a pattern as actionable. Beyond the low/high of the candle or base is a common starting point.

Pair with structure

Candles refine timing inside a zone (DBR, RBR, RBD, DBD). They do not replace bases, impulsive departures, or regime context.

How this ties to WINEMI structure work

  • Inside a demand base (DBR / RBR), rejection of lows (hammers, dragonfly, bullish engulfing, morning star) is timing detail — not a substitute for the base.
  • Inside a supply base (RBD / DBD), rejection of highs (shooting star, gravestone, bearish engulfing, evening star) plays the same role.
  • After an impulsive departure, marubozu-type bodies and three soldiers/crows often describe the impulse leg; doji clusters may describe pause.
  • Always write invalidation and check the calendar before treating any pattern as high quality.
